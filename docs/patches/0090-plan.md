# Patch 0090: the float report judges floats by simulation (device test T6, part 1)

Stacks on 0089.

## What you reported (T6)
> it values higher notes too heavily, I have no 20s and 10s, and it wants me to get 6 20s and 1 10 when I already have 106 in there. This doesn't seem like advanced enough of an algorithm either.

(The second half of your T6 note, entering cash from your own pocket and seeing what to exchange, is patch 0091.)

## What was wrong
1. **Size guess.** `idealFloat` assumed a float should hold 1.5 times a whole day's change. It ignored that every note a customer hands over goes back into the drawer and is used as change for the next one, so the target was far bigger than any real drawer needs.
2. **Ranked by dollar value.** The top-up list was sorted by (shortfall x note value), so $20s always went first and used up the whole gap.
3. **Shortlist from the wrong judge.** Choosing what to bank used a "coverage" score (can the drawer make each usual change amount once, exactly) that cannot see a drawer running out over a day. Found while testing: with a search cut short, it also only ever looked at ways that bank handfuls of coins, and chose a float with no $1 or $2 coins left (92% of imagined sales failing).

## How it works now
- The simulator from 0088 (`makeScript` and `play` in `src/lib/changeImpact.ts`) plays a day of imagined cash sales: the change amounts and the note handed over for each come from your profile (or item prices, or a generic spread). The same seeded script is played against every drawer being compared, so results are repeatable and differences are real. A day is twice the profile's horizon, 30 imagined days.
- `planTopUp` (`src/lib/floatPlan.ts`): start from the float that stays. Each step tries adding a note or coin (or a small handful: coins come in twos and fours because one coin alone often fixes nothing) and takes the step with the best (failure points saved) minus (0.04 points per dollar added). Ties go to the cheaper. Stops at 3% failures, when no step gains 0.5 points, at $200 added, after 20 steps, or after 1.5 s (best so far). Never $50 or $100.
- `rankTakeOuts`: every candidate way of banking the takings is played against a short day (10 imagined days); the best 30 against the full day; the winner has the lowest (failure points + 1.5 per dollar from the exact takings). The old quick score is gone from the report.
- `findCombinationsLimited` gets a `bigFirst` option (most of each note first). Same results when not cut short; when cut short it has covered the big-note ways first. The report uses it.
- The report shows: *about N% of a typical day's cash sales could get exact change* (and the drawer as it is now), and with a top-up *with the top-up, about M%; the float would be about $X*. A drawer that copes says *Nothing needs adding*.
- The sheet calculates after it draws (it says *Working it out...*), so a slow iPad shows a sheet straight away.

## Honest limits
- It is only as good as the profile. With few tracked cash sales it leans on item prices, and a spread of big change amounts makes big notes look necessary. The report says what it is based on at the bottom.
- I have not run this on a device. In my sandbox one report takes about 0.1 to 1 second; Hermes on an iPad is slower, which is why there is a 1.5 s cap on the top-up search.
- Top-up still adds money (from the bank or safe). Swapping your own cash for the drawer's surplus is patch 0091.
